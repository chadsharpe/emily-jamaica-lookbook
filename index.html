For Emily · 4–17 March 2027

Thirteen nights
by the water.

We land in Montego Bay. Then we live on the cliffs of Negril — limestone, thatch, ladders into water so clear you can see the fish from the table. Then Seven Mile Beach: pale sand, one of the great beaches in the world. Then four nights at Papaya Beach — private sand, height and ocean sunset, a quiet recovery north of the strip. Last night we dress up for dinner on the lagoon.

Chapter · Montego Bay

The bay
we land into.

Hip Strip light, Doctor’s Cave as a pale ribbon of sand, the Caribbean already in the room. One night high above the water — then west.

4 Thu

Afternoon · arrive & settle

We land at lunch.

Out of St. John’s before dawn, through Toronto, into Montego Bay at 12:35. A taxi brings us to the Hip Strip early afternoon — soft hours before dinner.

We do not swim yet. That is tomorrow morning.

Stay one · Montego Bay

Ocean View Penthouse

Hip Strip · Thursday night, Friday morning · A top-floor stay across from Doctor’s Cave.

A king suite with a private balcony that opens onto the bay: woven swing chair, glass-top table, the Hip Strip thinned to palms and turquoise. Inside — tufted leather headboard, teal accents, pendant lights, sliding doors that keep the sea in the room.

Closest stay to the airport. We arrive, we stand on the balcony, we walk to dinner on Gloucester.

4 Thu

Evening · Gloucester Avenue

Dinner by the water.

Marguerites is a short walk from the Penthouse — white linen, dark woven chairs, the sea at the edge of the table. Arrival night. Seafood. Soft light.

5 Fri

Morning · before we leave the bay

Swim, then west.

Morning at Doctor’s Cave — the beach we watched from above yesterday, now at water level. White sand, pale turquoise, a club on the Hip Strip. Then lunch at Pelican Grill, a few minutes up Gloucester: a welcome first proper meal before the island opens west.

Pelican Grill on Gloucester — coral facade, Bahama shutters, a Montego Bay classic since before we were born.

Friday afternoon

Private taxi across the island — about two hours. The road climbs, then drops toward the West End cliffs. We are not on the sand yet. We are going to live on limestone.

Chapter · Negril West End

Limestone,
thatch, ladders.

Circular roofs, stone stairs, water so clear the ladders look painted on. Five nights on this edge: first Little Bird, then Splashrock deeper toward the lighthouse.

Five nights on this edge begin at Little Bird — the first cottage that is ours.

Stay two · West End cliffs

Little Bird Cottage

High Cove · Friday afternoon through Saturday · Wooden louvers, thatch, a private limestone cove.

Designed for the breeze: louvers instead of glass, teak and stone underfoot, a thatch pavilion in cobalt that faces the open Caribbean. The architecture is the climate — shade, air, and a stair that ends in turquoise.

We cook Friday night. Saturday we walk the same road to lunch in a cave. Sunset on our own deck — not the circus down the road.

5 Fri

Night · arrive at High Cove

Ladder, cove, cook.

We arrive at Little Bird as the light softens. Wooden louvers, the cobalt thatch, the ladder into the limestone cove under the house. Further along the cliffs than town. Dinner at home — fish if we find it, vegetables, the sound of water under the rock.

Above the cove, the cobalt thatch pavilion is where the day gathers — coffee, a book, the late light.

6 Sat

Still at Little Bird

Lunch in a cave.

Xtabi sits on the same road, a little further toward the point. White thatch, turquoise rails, rooms of rock with the sea coming in. We eat there. Walk the caves.

Dinner we cook. Sunset on our deck — quiet, our own light, not Rick’s circus.

Down in the caves the Caribbean pushes through limestone rooms — cool shade after the cliff-top light.

Saturday night we sleep at Little Bird one last time — teak, mosquito net, louvers open to the sea air.

Sunday we move further west toward the lighthouse — same coast, quieter at night.

Chapter · Deep West

Rock pools
& ladders.

Further from town, toward the lighthouse. Louvers, a king bed, a bar that opens onto water. Three nights living between the bed, the bar, and the drop into turquoise.

Stay three · Deeper West End

Splashrock

Sunday, Monday, Tuesday · Three nights on the cliff edge — louvers, a king bed, a bar that opens onto the water.

Warm wood walls, full-height louvers, a bar built like a boat cabin that faces open sea. Live between the bed, the bar, and the ladders. Same coast as Little Bird, further from town, quieter at night.

7 Sun

Move further west

Deeper on the same cliffs.

A short hop toward the lighthouse. Splashrock waits with louvers and ladders. Lunch at Just Natural on the way — a garden place, vegetables and shade — then settle into the bar that opens onto the water. Lighthouse optional if the afternoon wants height.

Just Natural — thatched garden path, hand-painted signs, a lunch tucked into the greenery near the lighthouse road.

Sunday’s arrival ritual: drop in from the house — metal ladder, turquoise at the rock.

8 Mon

Still at Splashrock

A jump, then dinner.

Rick’s is a few minutes back toward town. People jump off a wooden platform into deep water. We go between two and four, while you can still swim and jump. By sunset the tour buses arrive and it becomes a crowd watching the sun. We don’t stay for that.

Dinner is at Rockhouse. Stone cottages, conical thatch, steps carved into the rock. We are diners, not guests.

Dinner is at Rockhouse — stone cottages, conical thatch, steps carved into the rock. We are diners, not guests.

The restaurant hangs over the same rock — thatch dining, limestone arches, the Caribbean as the fourth wall.

9 Tue

Last full day on the rocks

A morning loop along West End Road.

Optional and light: taxi from Splashrock along the cliffs to Joseph’s Cave — a limestone cave under the rock near Rockhouse. You swim in, and there is an optional jump if you want it. Then Barney’s Flower & Hummingbird Garden, where hummingbirds take sugar water from your hand. Back to Splashrock by midday.

Afternoon: ladders, or the hammock until the light goes gold. Evening: cook at home, or walk to Ivan’s at Catcha Falling Star if Tuesday wants a table that isn’t our kitchen — adults-only cliffs, thatch overhead, sea underneath.

Evening option: walk to Ivan’s at Catcha Falling Star — thatch overhead, sea underneath, no circus. Or cook.

We leave the cliffs. The coast opens into Long Bay — pale sand, shallow kind water, two nights on the beach.

Chapter · Seven Mile Beach

Long Bay.
Pale sand.

Seven miles of pale sand and a long, calm bay — one of the great beaches in the world. After five nights of limestone, the body understands sand again. Two nights sleeping on this shore.

Stay four · Seven Mile Beach

Beachfront Suite

Wednesday and Thursday · Two nights on the sand at Jamwest, about halfway along Seven Mile Beach.

A private king suite with a navy sofa, a kitchenette, and glass that looks straight at the bay. Modern, calm, designed for bare feet. Sliding doors to palms and turquoise.

10 Wed

Onto the sand

We sleep on the sand.

Out of Splashrock at noon. A local taxi up the West End and onto Seven Mile Beach. Beachfront Suite waits with sliding doors to palms and turquoise water.

Lunch at Rasta Ade on the way. Dinner at Miss Lily’s at Skylark — early seating keeps the beachside calm.

Rasta Ade on Norman Manley — ital plates, painted tables, feet in the sand of Seven Mile.

Wednesday evening: Miss Lily’s at Skylark — beachside, album covers, the sea through the louvers. Early seating if we want it quieter.

11 Thu

Still on Seven Mile Beach

Feet in the sand for lunch.

Walk the waterline. Swim until the light goes gold. Lunch at Cosmo’s — thatched seafood shack, picnic tables on the sand, stew fish and conch. Dinner we cook.

Cosmo’s at Long Bay Beach Park — vaulted timber, picnic tables, the Caribbean a few steps from your feet.

Tomorrow the coast softens into Papaya Beach — private sand, gardens, four nights of deliberate quiet.

Chapter · Papaya Beach

Private sand.
Recovery.

North of Negril at Green Island — Coconut Studio at Papaya Beach. Elevated ocean-sunset light, a vaulted wood studio, private sand below. Four nights of rest. We cook. We do not chase waterfalls.

Stay five · Papaya Beach

Coconut Studio

Friday through Monday · Four nights at Papaya Beach, Green Island — vaulted wood, balcony to the sunset, private sand.

A studio with a soaring plank ceiling, leaf-blade fan, and sliding doors that open onto a wooden balcony over the water. Queen bed, kitchenette, loft above. Outside: gardens, a saltwater pool, and a crescent of private sand that belongs to the place.

This is the quiet after the cliffs and the mile. We stay put on purpose.

12 Fri

Arrive at Papaya

Private sand and a high room.

After Seven Mile, a short taxi north to Green Island. Coconut Studio opens onto height and ocean. Private sand below. We cook. We settle in.

Inside: vaulted wood, leaf-blade fan, a studio made for long light and the balcony beyond.

13 Sat

Recovery after cliffs and sand

Nothing is booked on purpose.

Cook breakfast. Private sand. Pool if we want still water. Watch the sun go into the Caribbean from the balcony. No tours. No waterfall day. This is the quiet after the cliffs and the mile.

14 Sun

Stay put

Same sand, same light.

Same rhythm: private beach, balcony, cook when we want. The grounds are the outing.

15 Mon

Last full day at Papaya

The same water, one more time.

Sand. Pool. Balcony light. Pack lightly in the evening — tomorrow we go back to the bay.

The private sand below the studio — a crescent of pale shore, clear shallows, foliage to the edge.

Tuesday we go back to the bay — up the hill this time, one night at Vera before the plane.

Chapter · Back to the bay

Harbour light.
Last night.

Up the hill above downtown — white rooms, a balcony over harbour and sea. Afternoon in the square. Dinner floating on Bogue Lagoon.

Stay six · Back to the bay

Vera

Tuesday night, Wednesday morning · A modern king apartment up the hill — white rooms, mustard pillow, balcony over the harbour.

Clean lines, white walls, a living room that becomes outdoor dining when the doors slide open. Designed for the last night: dress up, go into town, come home to the harbour lights.

16 Tue

Last night

A last table on the lagoon.

Private taxi in the late morning, back to the bay, this time up the hill. Afternoon: Sam Sharpe Square and the National Gallery West. Dinner at HouseBoat Grill — a floating restaurant on Bogue Lagoon. Taxi from Vera. The last-night table.

Then dinner at HouseBoat Grill — a converted boat on Bogue Lagoon, green trim, lights on still water.

We ask for upstairs when we can — teak, louvers, quieter and closer to the night air.

We come back up the hill to Vera — quiet white bedroom, the harbour still out there in the dark.

17 Wed

Home

Brunch, then the plane.

Pelican Grill on Gloucester — a welcome return to the same lunch we loved before we went west. Taxi at 10:15 for the afternoon flight.
